# Web page

**App name:** No Excuses

## Headline
Cut your habit spending in half. No excuses.

## Subhead
Pick the two habits draining your money. Log every buy in seconds. Your weekly limit drops every four weeks, hit or miss, until you're spending half of what you started with.

**Trust line under the button:** Free · No account · No bank login · Data stays on your phone.

## The problem, in the user's words
"Honestly, I have no clue what I spend on pizza. I go two to four times a week, it's like twenty bucks each time, and it never feels like a lot, so I just don't add it up. Same with cash. I cut back once because the people close to me got on me about it, but nobody's doing that for pizza."

Shown next to two numbers:
- **$260–$600 a month.** The student's own estimate, and they can't say which end of the range is true.
- **43% of college students surveyed say they don't track their monthly spending.** Source: LendEDU, 2016 College Students and Personal Finance Study, 455 students: https://lendedu.com/blog/college-students-and-personal-finance-study. This is the only research claim on the page, and it was verified word for word.

## How the app helps
1. **You see the real number.** Spend the first four weeks just logging, so you learn what you actually spend instead of guessing.
2. **You feel the limit before you buy.** The app turns your weekly limit into "1 pizza buy left," and the resist button counts the buys you walk away from.
3. **The limit drops whether you're ready or not.** Every four weeks it falls by 10% of your starting number, until you're at half. Missing a target doesn't slow it down.

## Page sections, in order
1. **Hero:** the headline, the subhead, the "Start your plan" button, the trust line, and a phone mockup of Home with a live resist countdown. Two floating cards sit beside the phone: "You held off +$21" and "$19 pizza = 1.3 hours of work."
2. **The problem:** a dark section with the quote and the two numbers.
3. **How it works:** three steps (name your two habits → log for four weeks → watch the limit drop) and an animated bar ladder: 100 (measure) → 90 → 80 → 70 → 60 → 50 (goal). The note under it: "Miss a block? The next one drops anyway."
4. **What's inside:** the resist button as a large tile, with a working demo. The demo clock shows the real 10:00 countdown at 60× speed (10:00 → 9:59 → …, about 10 seconds total), labeled as sped up. Then a 3 × 3 grid: buys left (with the pace warning), the accountability text (with a sample message including "Why:"), cost in hours, streaks and money kept, private by design, your why, and works with no signal (plus Undo).
   - **Mockup numbers must match the app's math:** Pizza "$22 of $54 left · 1 buy left" (about a $21 average), Cash "$30 of $36 left · 2 buys left" (about a $15 average).
5. **Why not a budgeting app?** A comparison table, "Typical budgeting app" vs. No Excuses: what it tracks, setup, when it helps, cash purchases, the plan, and buys you didn't make. It names no competitors and makes no claim about any specific product.
6. **Do the math:** sliders for buys per week, cost per buy and hourly pay. It shows the yearly cost, the hours of work, and what you'd keep at half. Everything is calculated in the browser.
7. **Questions:** bank connection, where the data lives, going over the limit, holidays, working without signal, logging the wrong amount, cost, and whether you need an app store.
8. **Final call to action:** "Start with the last one you bought," with the "Start your plan" button.

## What to do next
Open the app, name your two habits and pick a Monday. The classmate test task is: log a pizza buy and find how many buys are left, in under 30 seconds.

## Look and feel
- **Tone:** direct and a little tough, like a coach, not a bank. Short sentences. No finance jargon and no exclamation points.
- **Colors:** an off-white background (#FAF7F2), near-black text (#1A1A1A), and one accent color: tomato red (#D7392B) for buttons, the "buys left" number and anything over the limit. Green (#1F8A4C) is used only for wins and "saved."
- **Font:** Inter. Headlines are heavy (900) with tight letter-spacing.
- **Layout:** two columns on desktop and a single column on phones. Sections fade up as you scroll.
- **Image:** a phone mockup built in code that matches the real app. No stock photos of people or money, and no pizza clip art.
- **Honesty rule:** no claim the page can't back up. Each research number links to its source. "Typical budgeting app" claims are general, never about a named product.
