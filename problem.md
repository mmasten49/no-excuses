# The problem

## Who has it
A college student whose only non-essential weekly spending is two habits: pizza and cash purchases. Everything else they spend money on is a necessity. The problem happens every time they make one of those purchases. They buy pizza 2–4 times a week at $13–26 a trip, and they make cash purchases through the week. They never stop to add it up.

## What it costs them
Money, and they don't know how much. Rough estimates:
- Pizza: 2–4 trips a week × $13–26 = about $26–104 a week, or $113–450 a month.
- Cash purchases: about $150 a month. This is a guess.
- Total: about $260–600 a month, or $3,100–7,200 a year.

The range is wide because nobody is tracking it. Not knowing the real number is part of the cost.

## How they handle it today
From memory. They try to keep a running tally in their head. There is no written record, no budget, and no weekly limit.

Every purchase is made in person. They don't use delivery apps or buy online, so no app or website keeps an order history of these purchases.
- **Pizza is paid by card.** Each purchase appears on their bank transaction statement, but nothing adds those charges up or tags them as pizza.
- **Cash purchases happen 1–2 times a week.** They leave no record anywhere, but they are rare enough to remember and log by hand.

## Why that falls short
- Memory doesn't add numbers up. They can't say what they spent last week, let alone last month.
- There's no limit and no time window, so nothing tells them to stop for the week. Spending comes in clumps instead of being spread out.
- No single purchase is big enough to sting. Each one feels small, so there's never a moment that makes them notice.
- **No one is holding them accountable.** They have cut back before: cash spending used to be double what it is now. That worked because a parent and a close friend kept telling them to tone it back, and they committed to it. Nobody does that for pizza, so nothing pushes back.

## How they would know it is solved
**A week** runs from Monday 8:00 AM to the next Monday 8:00 AM. Weekly limits reset at that moment.

**A "month" is a 4-week block**, not a calendar month. Block 1 is weeks 1–4, block 2 is weeks 5–8, and so on. Every block starts on a Monday at 8:00 AM.

1. **Block 1 is the measurement block.** They track every pizza and cash purchase and cut nothing. The block-1 total for each category is the starting number.
2. **Every block after that, spending drops 10 points off the starting number:** block 2 at 90%, block 3 at 80%, block 4 at 70%, block 5 at 60%, block 6 at 50%.
   - **Tough love: no repeats.** If a block misses its target, the next block drops anyway.
3. **Weekly limit** for each category = (starting number ÷ 4) × that block's percentage.
   - Example: block-1 pizza total of $240 → $60 a week. Block 2 at 90% = a $54 weekly limit.
4. **Solved = spending is at or below 50% of the starting number in block 6** (counted weeks 21–24), checked block by block.
   - Break weeks like Thanksgiving or Christmas can be skipped. They don't count, and the block runs one week longer.
5. **Ideal end state:** 1–2 pizza trips a week, and about $25 a week on cash purchases.

If a block misses its target, the app says so, but the schedule doesn't slow down. Missing a target is a signal to try harder, not a reason to ease off.
